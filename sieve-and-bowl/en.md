# The Sieve and the Bowl

<small>[nonasking](https://github.com/nonasking) · 2026-11 · [한국어판](../)</small>

<small>*This essay was written with heavy use of AI. Exactly how is disclosed at the end.*</small>

<small>*This essay shares its premises with ["Sensitivity"](../../sensitivity/en/). It can be read on its own.*</small>

You open the door and walk in. Two people stop talking. One of them laughs. "Is it about me?" You sit down.

You have not slept in three days. You open the door and walk in. Two people stop talking. One of them laughs. "It is about me."

## The Mouth

When a bowl is being made, its mouth is open at first. Drawing it in comes later. A person is the same. At first everything comes in.

What repeats and what does no harm are the first to be left outside, and some of it is made into the rim (口緣). Over the years the rim thickens and the mouth narrows.

A sensitive person is a bowl whose mouth has stayed wide. Through a wide mouth more comes in. The stopped talk and the laugh, the length of the laugh, the glance that passed before it.

## Linking

Many people link what comes in to themselves. Whether that laugh was about them, whether that glance was meant for them. When about twelve hundred university students were asked, one in four said that at least once a week they had the thought that people were communicating about them in subtle ways.

For an animal living in a group, this was useful for survival. The animal that took another's growl to be about itself was the first to run. It is an immunity of the mind. For survival a false negative was more dangerous than a false positive, so this immunity leans toward false alarms.

A wide mouth takes in a great deal. There is much to link. Even what a narrow mouth would have left outside comes in and becomes material.

## The Sieve

Sorting what has come in is a separate job. What is relevant from what is not, what was meant for oneself from what was only passing. Sifting takes a sieve.

The sieve stays at "it may be so, and it may not." A coincidence. Curious. Strange. These words link without closing. The same link is a thought when it stays open as "it may be so" and a delusion when it closes as "it must be so." The shape of the conviction separates the two.

In a sensitive person the mouth is wide and the sieve is fine. A wide mouth takes in much, and a fine sieve sorts finely.

## The Bowl

The bowl supports the sieve. The body is the bowl.

In one experiment eighteen people were kept awake for a day and shown faces. On the day without sleep they read more of the faces as threats.

Put together the older experiments that kept people awake longer, and there is an order. Past one day, what is seen distorts. Past two days, things that are not there are seen and heard. Past three days, delusions come. With sleep, in many cases, it went back.

The bowl is what the sieve stands on. When the bowl is damaged, the sieve tilts.

Tending the bowl does not guarantee that the sieve holds. In a trial that split more than thirty-seven hundred university students with insomnia into two groups and treated the sleep of only one, suspicion and hallucinations both went down, but not by much.

But when the bowl is not tended, the sieve tilts. Without sleep, within two days what is seen begins to distort. Push the bowl hard and the sieve can be tilted in a single stroke. Returning a long-tilted sieve and a disordered bowl to how they were takes a great deal of work.

## What Wears Away

What could not be sorted stays. What stays wears away sleep. Even after lying down, the stopped talk and the laugh replay. In some people it wears away eating as well.

Anxiety turns the sieve toward its own feed. What feeds the anxiety comes through in greater number and at greater size. People who were made anxious grew more suspicious, and twice as many of them reached a conclusion on little evidence.

Worn sleep damages the bowl, the sieve tilts on the damaged bowl, and what the tilted sieve has sorted wrongly wears away sleep again.

## The Tilted Sieve

What happens in a tilted sieve is set down here.

One is that the sieve tilts inside the bowl, and what should have been sorted goes past the side of the sieve unsorted. "It may be so, and it may not" becomes "it must be so." What would have been a coincidence closes into necessity, what would have been curious into fate, what would have been strange into providence.

The other is that what the bowl has caught is poured back onto the sieve. What has already come through cannot be closed, and it is poured onto the same sieve again. Pour the same thing onto the same sieve and the same thing comes through as before. The more this is repeated, the harder it becomes to close.

Closing and being unable to close can both happen in one tilted sieve.

## Making

Among the ways of sending out what has come in, there are ways that make something.

Talk and song send it out. Exercise sends it out through the body and leaves strength and stamina behind. As it sends things out, it makes the bowl itself.

Making things takes what has come in, makes something else of it, and sends that out. It can also wear down the maker's own bowl. The way by which the made thing is born and leaves runs through the bowl. That is the pain of birth (産苦).

Things are also made from what came past unsifted. Their shape differs from that of things made from the sifted.

## Support

You have slept. You open the door and walk in. Two people stop talking. One of them laughs. "Is it about me?" You sit down.

A question holds something up over its full stop. Holding it up takes support.
