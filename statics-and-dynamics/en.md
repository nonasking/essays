# On Aesthetics as Statics and as Dynamics

<small>[nonasking](https://github.com/nonasking) · 2026-09 · [한국어판](../)</small>

<small>*This essay was written with heavy use of AI. Exactly how is disclosed at the end.*</small>

<small>*This essay shares its premises with the series ["Heliotropism."](../../heliotropism/en/) It can be read on its own.*</small>

## Two Portraits

Dorian Gray took the time out of his own face and handed it to a portrait. The face stayed; the painting aged. Rembrandt painted his own face close to eighty times over forty years, from twenty-two to sixty-three. The smooth young face, the swollen and reddened face, the face just after bankruptcy, the sagging and heavy face. The last self-portrait is the face of the year he died.

One man froze a portrait; the other used portraits to record time. The endings are known. Dorian stabbed the portrait and died himself. Rembrandt's faces hang scattered across museums, and set in a row they make forty years.

What divides the two faces is neither talent nor morals. It is whether beauty is seen as a state or as a trajectory. This essay is about that one distinction.

## The Equation Without Time

Mechanics divides in two. Statics is the physics of forces in equilibrium, of things at rest. A bridge stands, an arch holds, a scale comes to a stop. Dynamics is the physics of forces producing motion. The curve of a thrown stone, a pendulum swinging down to stillness. The difference between the two disciplines comes down to one letter. The equations of statics have no time variable. There is no t in them.

The eye that sees beauty divides the same way. The aesthetics of statics sees beauty in a completed state. A pose, a single picture, a flawless surface. The aesthetics of dynamics sees beauty in a trajectory. Movement, departure, and return. The first eye reads its object as a constant; the second reads it as a variable.

What the two eyes face must be set down first. The absolute, commonly represented as beauty, or goodness, or truth, is not something that can be reached, only something that can be faced. If it can be reached, it is not the absolute. The aesthetics of statics uses this absolute as a coordinate. It asks whether the object coincides with it. The aesthetics of dynamics uses this absolute as a direction. It asks whether the object is moving toward it. So in dynamics, return is not arrival. It is turning a trajectory that had strayed back toward the absolute.

In aesthetics as statics, the beautiful thing is the thing that does not waver. The thing that does not leave its coordinate. In aesthetics as dynamics, the beautiful thing is the thing that wavers and comes back. Not zero displacement, but a restoring force.

In statics, goodness is coincidence with the coordinate. Sin is a shortfall from that coincidence, and its measure is the size of the shortfall and its irreversibility. How far did the act fall from the coordinate, and can it be erased. These are static properties of the act itself, so there is no time term. In dynamics, goodness is a trajectory toward the absolute. A wrong is a departure from that trajectory, and its measure is the trajectory after the departure. Did it turn back. The equation has a return term. Just as goodness lies at one end, evil lies at the other. If goodness is the limit of approach, evil is the limit of departure, and both are ends of a direction, not coordinates. So in dynamics no act is goodness itself, and none is evil itself. There is only displacement along a direction.

Specimens of the unwavering being usually come from film. They have to. A character has no nervous system. A written being has no viscera to tremble. Everything alive vibrates. What physiology calls homeostasis is not a resting state but ceaseless correction, and even standing still is a control loop that never stops falling and catching itself. Complete stillness is not a property of life. It is a property of a corpse.

So measure a living person with the ruler of statics and everyone falls short. This is not a defect in the person; it is the specification of the ruler. A tool without time was used on an object with time, that is all. And if the far end of that ruler is marked with the absolute, the shortfall is permanent by definition. A ruler whose coordinate is something unreachable has no mark for arrival.

## Constants and Variables

Here one wants to indict statics, and the temptation must be resisted for this essay to stand. There is no ranking between the two aesthetics. Rank them and this becomes one more essay on perfectionism; refuse to, and it becomes an estimate.

Statics pays in accuracy and saves on labor. It fixes the object in a single snapshot, so the calculation is cheap, and it is wrong whenever the object moves or the angle changes. Dynamics pays in labor and buys accuracy. It holds the object as a variable, so it must recalculate every time, and it is wrong less often. Not never wrong. Less often. Wittgenstein wrote that the eye is not in the visual field. Even the self in one's own field of view is a snapshot.

Dynamics does not refrain from taking snapshots. It takes many and reads the differences. Dynamics is the derivative of snapshots. So the labor of dynamics is exactly the number of shots taken.

And treating something as a constant is automatic, while treating it as a variable is a discipline. Psychology calls it the halo effect when one beautiful part sets the impression of the whole, and the fact that it has a name means it is the default. The brain is, from birth, what this essay will call a statician: a creature of statics. Dynamics has to be learned, and once learned it has to be paid for every time.

There is no question of which is right, only of where each is used. Holding a passerby as a variable is waste; holding a spouse as a constant turns the marriage into a snapshot. The stakes decide.

But where the savings get billed should be written down. The moment an object held as a constant moves, the prediction breaks, and blaming the object for the broken prediction is what betrayal feels like. "You've changed." Much of that accusation is an accounting error by the observer. Nothing changed. What was always a variable had been entered as a constant.

There is one pathology on each side, symmetrically. The pathology of statics is mistaking the snapshot for the real thing. Bergson called this the cinematographic mechanism of thought: the habit of the intellect that cuts what moves into still frames and then believes that stringing the frames together yields motion. The snapshot itself is no sin. Forgetting that the snapshot is a snapshot is the disease. The pathology of dynamics is the infinite deferral of judgment. Hold everything as a variable and nothing gets settled, and what does not get settled becomes a blank page. The disease of taking a constant for the real, and the disease of never being able to set a constant down. To be honest, this essay has to write both side by side.

One clause of reconciliation. Dynamics has constants too. The pendulum's equation has a restoring coefficient, and it does not change. Dynamics does not discard constants; it moves them from the state to the parameters. "This is who he is" is a constant of state. "This is how he comes back" is a constant of parameter. The second is still a constant. It simply carries time inside it, and is that much more supple.

## The Budget of Contemplation

There are in fact three modes, not two. Statics, dynamics, and the third: not contemplating at all.

To contemplate someone's shape is itself labor. Dynamics is a large labor and statics a small one, but even storing a snapshot takes capacity. So the way to reduce the labor is not to freeze the objects but to reduce the number of objects contemplated. To whatever lies outside contemplation, one responds with rules alone, without a model.

Game theory has a field measurement of this mode. When Axelrod held his tournament of the iterated prisoner's dilemma, the strategy that scored highest, ahead of the ones that modeled their opponents with care, was the simplest. Tit for tat. It asks nothing about who the opponent is, what he wants, or what he lives toward. It answers his last move by rule, nothing more. Axelrod listed four properties behind the win. It never defects first. It retaliates at once. Having retaliated, it forgets. And it is easy to read: the opponent can grasp the rule immediately. Clarity was a condition of victory. There is one known failure. If a single defection or a single misreading sets it off, both sides get locked in a loop of nothing but retaliation. Echo. So later work produced a generous tit for tat that lets the first slip go.

A label is the ticket into contemplation. The moment you label someone, the moment you ask what he lives toward, that person is already inside the contemplated set. Cheaper than a label is a rule.

So there are three modes. The contemplated variable, the constant that reduces contemplation to a single shot, and the rule with no contemplation at all. The choice between statics and dynamics arises only inside the contemplated set; outside it is neither. Contemplation is a scarce resource, and the list of those who receive it is the budget of the one who contemplates.

## The Label One Pins on Oneself

What happens when statics turns on the self. From here on, this is an essay about perfectionism.

"Am I a beautiful person?" The moment one tries to answer with a binary label, the error is booked. Everyone has beautiful parts and ugly parts side by side. So label the whole being and there are only two exits: since there are ugly parts, be an ugly person; or pretend not to see them, and be arrogant. The second is no comfort either. The person knows he is not the absolute. Arrogance breeds anxiety.

Psychology has already separated these two circuits. In Tangney's decades of research, a label pinned on the being is shame and a label pinned on the act is guilt. "I am a bad person" and "I did a bad thing." Guilt-proneness leads to empathy, apology, repair, and correction; shame-proneness leads to hiding, avoidance, and the externalizing of blame. The same wrong yields an apology or a counterattack depending on which circuit receives it.

Now perfectionism can be defined. Perfectionism is statics applied to the absolute. In the language of mathematics, it is substituting a limit into a constant.

Only a variable can approach a limit. A constant either equals the limit or differs from it. So the perfectionist only ever receives the verdict "differs." Statics that reads a person as a constant is economy; statics that reads the absolute as a constant is error. The absolute is by definition a direction, not a coordinate, and a direction admits no coincidence. Setting the absolute in the constant's place and registering it to one's own being is perfectionism. So the perfectionist's anxiety is not a temperament but an unavoidable piece of accounting. He is anxious because he knows the registration is void.

The affliction has two examples.

First, the inability to apologize. An apology is the utterance of someone who can admit a wrong without collapsing, and that muscle belongs only to those who pin labels on parts. For someone who pins them on the being, admitting an ugly part brings the being down, so the most he can produce is regret. Regret is a feeling about outcomes; an apology is the acceptance of an act. Perfectionism is an aesthetics that cannot apologize.

Second, forgery. When beauty is a direction, it produces sanding: acknowledge the rough spot and smooth it. When beauty becomes a possession, it produces defense. A position must be defended, defense demands the destruction of counterevidence, and failure is counterevidence. Mueller and Dweck reproduced this with a single sentence. Children solved problems; one group was praised for being smart, a praise of the being, and one for working hard, a praise of the process. Then they were given problems hard enough to fail and asked to report their scores to an anonymous peer. Thirty-eight percent of the children praised for their being inflated their scores; among those praised for the process, thirteen percent. Plant one constant and the rate of forgery after failure nearly tripled. The trait of neither displaying nor voicing one's flaws is what clinicians call perfectionistic self-presentation, and they measure it on its own. Self-forgiveness research produces the same ingredient list. Genuine self-forgiveness is made of accepting responsibility, remorse, restitution where possible, and separating the act from one's worth. Pseudo self-forgiveness is made of denying responsibility, blaming the victim, and belittling the victim. In an aesthetics that prices confession at death, the system converges on forgery. Whoever faces beauty gains growth rings; whoever tries to own it gains perjury.

Only three kinds of being pass the ruler of perfection. Characters, corpses, forgers. That ruler cannot produce a strong human; it produces only forgers and the despairing.

Virgil, dying, asked that the *Aeneid* be burned. It was unfinished. Augustus refused, and the unfinished poem became canon. Gogol put the second part of *Dead Souls* into the fire with his own hands. Virgil pinned a constant to his manuscript and Augustus read it as a variable, and the world has the poem thanks to Augustus. Perfectionism sometimes puts out with its own hand the light it gave birth to.

## Nodes

Since the equations of statics have no t, it is not a defect but a definition that the aesthetics of statics cannot handle aging. Look at an object with time through a tool without time, and aging is misread as ugliness.

This does not mean dynamics denies decline. Winter is a loss; that much is true. But loss and ugliness are quantities of different kinds. Loss is the decrease of a variable; ugliness is a label. Diminishing and changing direction are different things. A dimmed lamp can still face the same way, and the absolute is pursued by direction, not by brightness, so decline is not a departure from orbit. Dynamics reads the decrease and pins no label. It need not insist that winter is summer, and so it becomes honest. Winter is a loss, not an ugliness.

There is one plant that fits this axis of time. Bamboo. Bamboo is a monocot, so its stem never thickens. Unlike a tree, which stacks time inward, bamboo stacks time only upward, and the record of that is its nodes. And a bamboo culm grows to its full height in a single season and never gains another inch. One node is one step of height, and a node denied does not come back. What is more, the node looks like the weakest point on the stem, and structurally it is the opposite. A hollow culm does not snap because of its nodes. The sparse seasons hold the structure up.

The moment one denies the tender nodes of one's own past and cuts oneself down, what remains is not a tree but taxidermy. That is the perfectionist's self-image.

Chinese characters wrote this section in a single glyph long ago. Node, 節. Integrity, 節槪, is written with it, and that is why the Four Gentlemen of ink painting include bamboo. In the Four Gentlemen, bamboo holds the place of winter. And season, 季節, is the node of the year. To keep all of one's nodes is integrity; to keep one's winter is a season.

Rembrandt painted his nodes. Dorian cut his off.

## Limbs

The aesthetics of statics has two exits. Freeze yourself, or cut someone else. Dorian took the first. The stories of those who take the second are older, and there are more of them.

The possessor is a statician. To him, beauty is a pose. A fixed image. And limbs are the organs of movement, and movement is the possibility of leaving. To the statician's eye, limbs are not part of the beauty but parts that can damage it. So within his aesthetics, cutting is not destruction but completion.

History holds proof that this is not madness. For a thousand years one civilization broke women's feet so they could not walk properly, and the broken foot was defined as the highest beauty. What could not move was the definition of the beautiful. Fowles's *The Collector* is the story of a man who collected butterflies and then collected a woman. The beauty of a pinned butterfly is not in its wings. It is in wings that do not fly. What Annie in *Misery* loves is not Paul but the Paul who keeps writing, and when she learns he has been out of his room, she cuts off his foot. The foot was the one organ that threatened her access.

Proust wrote down the paradox of this circuit. Lock Albertine up and the love goes cold. You fix her because you want her, and the moment she is fixed the reason you wanted her is gone. What comes next is contempt or the next object. The beauty they saw was never in the face. It was in the possibility of monopoly.

"If it can be reached, it is not the absolute," this essay said earlier. What the possessor does is execute that sentence in reverse. Take what drew you because it could not be reached, and carve it until it can be. Once carved, it is no longer on the side of the absolute, and the reason it drew you is gone.

There is one exception, and it opens the door to the next section. In Tanizaki's *A Portrait of Shunkin*, when the blind beauty Shunkin loses her face to scalding, her disciple Sasuke puts out his own eyes. To fix her beauty inside himself. Instead of cutting the other, he cut himself to preserve the image. No novel shows more honestly that beauty was never in the other's face but in the image within. And he laid no hand on her body. A statician who paid with his own body.

## Jurisdiction

It is no accident that statics tends to take others' bodies as material while dynamics leans toward paying with one's own. Stillness can be forced on another; return cannot. Freezing is something that can be done from outside; coming back can only be done from within. Which eye you look with decides whose body becomes the cost.

Each person's aesthetics is his own. What is beautiful and what is ugly is decided in one's own sky. The error is not in the verdict but only in enforcing that verdict in someone else's sky.

Urmson drew this line in his paper on saints and heroes. Acts beyond duty can be praised but cannot be demanded, and a common error of morality is to promote the supererogatory into the obligatory. This is why the person willing to sacrifice himself for his aesthetics is, of all people, the one who comes to expect it of others. The same depth runs in two directions. In this essay's terms, it is the difference between spending one's own time with one's own hand and erasing someone else's. The first is a trajectory; the second is an amputation.

The oldest record of that amputation is Agamemnon. He sacrificed his daughter so the fleet could have its wind. Read in the grammar of the previous section, he is a statician. The coordinates of his aesthetics were honor and the fleet, and to hold those coordinates he erased another person's time. A sacrifice is the limit of stillness. As the one who cuts limbs removes movement, he removed the whole of his daughter's remaining time to fix his own coordinate. Kierkegaard called him a tragic hero, but read by jurisdiction the verdict is simple. The war was his; the body was hers.

What is interesting is that the tradition felt the hole. Euripides gave Iphigenia a last-minute consent of her own. The girl who begged for her life walks to the altar by herself. Watch what consent changes. With it, the death becomes a turn the body's owner made inside her own trajectory; without it, it is something another erased from outside. The tradition tried to rewrite an amputation as a trajectory. That consent was stitched in after the fact is evidence that not even the tradition could bear a stillness without consent.

Reckon by who pays for the aesthetics and a table appears. Pay with another's body: trespass. A hand laid on someone else's jurisdiction. Pay with your own: self-rule. Begun in your own jurisdiction and ended there. Pay with your own and hand the yield to another: a gift. The yield crosses into the other's jurisdiction, but whether to take it is for the receiver to decide. Goodness is none of the three; it is the direction all three can face. Even trespass can face goodness. Agamemnon believed he faced it. But facing it from your own jurisdiction and facing it from someone else's are different things. This table is why Sasuke, from the previous section, falls in the second row. The person who lives by his own aesthetics stands nearest to both extremes of good and evil. This is a statement not about position but about range. Whoever lives by borrowed norms cannot leave the middle. Only the self-legislator reaches both ends.

Whoever can reach both ends must measure, every time, which way he is turning now. The instrument for that is called conscience. And conscience too has two modes. The constant-type conscience waits for ratification; the equation-type goes to repair. The Kleinians divided this into persecutory guilt and depressive guilt: the guilt that worries about punishment and approval, and the guilt that knows it has done harm and goes toward reparation. The same conscience, and one produces anxiety while the other produces work.

## Side by Side

Apply dynamics to a relationship and it becomes the problem of ambivalence.

Statics computes a resultant. "I loved and I hated, but in the end it was love," "in the end it was hate." Add the vectors, produce a single remainder, and pin that remainder on as a label. Dynamics does not add. It sets them side by side.

Klein wrote this as two positions in development. In the paranoid-schizoid position the infant splits the good object from the bad and mounts each as a constant. One snapshot per object, all good or all bad. In the depressive position the infant bears the fact that the object it hated is the same person as the object it loves. What Klein called integration is not merging the feelings into one. It is keeping the object one while leaving the feelings two. No summation. Only the object stays whole.

There is one test sentence. Do both feelings attach to the same person at the same moment.

"Grateful when things are good, hurt when they are bad" is not yet there. The object looks like one, but there is a frame for each time, so in fact it is two snapshots. "When" is the buffer. It lets you set gratitude down for the hurtful moments and set the hurt down for the grateful ones. That is why it hurts less, and why it is still splitting. "I love him but I hate him" is not it either. "But" is a summation in which one side discounts the other.

The sentence that stands has this form. "I love him and I hate him."

One object, two feelings, no when, present tense. "And" is the juxtaposition that leaves both whole. The sentence is expensive because neither side can be set down. Since nothing gets mounted, there is no end, and since there is no end, the capacity keeps draining. When Klein called the depressive position painful, she meant exactly these two costs. Capacity, and mourning for the lost ideal object. Splitting is economical: one snapshot per object. Integration has to hold the entire time series for each. That is why it is hard, and why the contemplated set must be small. Whole objects are expensive, so one holds a few whole and lives by rule with the rest.

Bion saw the two positions not as stages but as an oscillation. Returning to splitting for a while is not regression but load management. This clause is what keeps the no-ranking rule intact. The problem is not which side you are on but taking up permanent residence on either.

One word needs a warning. Rumination. Clinically, rumination is the repeated failure to compute a resultant, and it is the pathology of dynamics under another name, the infinite deferral of judgment. Juxtaposition is not rumination. Juxtaposition is the state of having stopped trying to compute the resultant, and it is complete in itself.

## Acceptance

Apply dynamics to a wrong and it becomes the problem of forgiveness. And here a word has to be corrected.

Forgiveness without apology is not forgiveness but acceptance. This is this essay's distinction. Forgiveness needs the other party, and what one does alone, without the other's acknowledgment, needs a different name. But acceptance is not resignation. Resignation drops the verdict; acceptance keeps the verdict and drops only the demand.

The structure is borrowed from Hieronymi. On her account, resentment is not a mere feeling but a protest, and it asserts three things. "That act was wrong." "That person is a responsible agent." "I am not someone who may be treated that way." The easy way to let go of resentment is to give up one of the three. "It wasn't really wrong" is an excuse; "he couldn't help it" is an exemption; "who am I to mind" is self-abasement. What she calls uncompromising forgiveness is letting the resentment go while holding all three, and the clearest path by which that becomes possible, in her view, is the wrongdoer's repentance. Holding the same structure where no apology comes: that is what this essay calls acceptance. Experimental psychology has named the opposite. The doormat effect. Forgive someone who has done nothing, dropping the verdict along with the demand, and self-respect and self-concept clarity erode. Keeping the verdict and dropping only the protest is dignity; dropping the verdict too is servility.

The form of acceptance has four lines. First, the verdict. "That act was wrong." Second, the wish. "I wanted acknowledgment and reconciliation." Third, frustration and withdrawal. "That was frustrated for these reasons, and for these reasons I no longer demand it." Fourth, acceptance. "So I hold the tension."

There is nothing after the fourth line. Wishing the other well is a step further, and there is no guarantee that step is progress. The demand that forgiveness include a blessing is a trespass against the victim. Acceptance is the accounting that closes an act. Wishing a person well belongs to a different ledger, and that ledger need not be opened.

Why this form is dynamics should be written down. To keep the verdict is to keep the direction. The verdict that the act was wrong is the record of which way one's aesthetics faces, and to set it down is to lose the orbit. To withdraw the protest is to remove the other from the conditions of that orbit. The direction holds whether or not the other's acknowledgment arrives. Return needs no one's permission.

And this form is the bamboo of the earlier section. If pseudo self-forgiveness cuts the node away, acceptance preserves it. Leave the tender node where it is and grow upward. Do not cut out the verdict that it was wrong; set the next node on top of it. To live holding the tension means exactly that. That is the price of dynamics, and only the culm that has paid it does not snap.

## The One Who Returns

The statician cuts limbs; the dynamicist returns. Returns to what. Not to the absolute; the absolute cannot be reached. What returns is the trajectory that faced it. Looking that way again after wavering, turning that way again after a wrong, growing that way again after winter. Whether one returns to rightness or to beauty, it is the same verb. And the muscle of that return is strength.

Return requires limbs that could have left. What is frozen cannot return. It never left.

There is no ranking between the two eyes. Statics is cheap and dynamics is expensive, and there is only the question of where each is used. But there are two errors. Opening an equation without t before an object that has time, and marking as a coordinate what cannot be reached. The two meet in perfectionism. A person is an object that has time. He ages, wavers, does wrong, and loves while hating. Read that object by its distance from the absolute and everything alive falls short; read it by its trajectory toward the absolute and everything alive is a candidate.

The instrument of statics is the scale and the instrument of dynamics is the pendulum. A scale at rest measures weight; a pendulum in motion measures time. A pendulum becomes a clock not because it swings but because it comes back after swinging. A pendulum that does not come back is not a clock. It is a plumb bob.
