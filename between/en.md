# Between

<small>[nonasking](https://github.com/nonasking) · 2026-11 · [한국어판](../)</small>

<small>*This essay was written with heavy use of AI. Exactly how is disclosed at the end.*</small>

<small>*This essay shares its premises with ["Weight and Ties: On Hunger"](../../weight-and-ties/en/) and ["Being"](../../being/en/). It can be read on its own.*</small>

What follows describes the relation between beings. There are three things: distance, a line, and a third thing. A bond (紐帶) is a cord and a band. In everyday use the word means being closely tied. Here it is used for two beings tied together to a third thing. Contemplation (觀照) is seeing the other without altering it.

## Distance

The first variable between two beings is distance. At the two ends of distance are contemplation and union.

What contemplation sees is shape and movement. Shape is where the other is concave and where it is convex. Movement (動勢) is the shape of how it moves.

Union is distance reaching zero, so that the frames of two beings interlock. The concave part of one receives the convex part of the other. Union goes by two names. Seen from the bumps and dents (凹凸) it is interlocking; seen from the surfaces that touch it is lying against each other. They are two names for one event, read once as rubbing and once as touching.

What is seen changes with distance. From far away the whole shape and the movement are seen; up close the grain is seen. In union the interlocked surface is covered, and because the two move together, the other's movement is not seen. In union one cannot see the whole of the other. With palms pressed together one cannot read the other's palm. In union the way to know the interlocked place is not the eye but touch. Two at a distance cannot withstand by each other's weight. Where there is distance, the other's weight is over there. One is the place of seeing and one is the place of holding. As the distance between beings changes, they can go back and forth between union and contemplation.

## The Line

The second variable is the line: beings joined by a line. A moored boat does not drift when a wave comes. The line it is tied by goes taut. When one side shakes, the other receives only as much as travels along the line. Being tied by a line is not union. The axis of distance and the axis of ties stand apart.

When one is tied, the tied one moves as the other moves. This is subordination. Subordination is not a matter of high and low but the direction of a tie. The question is not who stands above but whose movement passes to whom. Tied to each other, two are subordinate to each other. The two lines need not be made of the same thing. One may be tied by this and the other by that. When only one is tied, only one is subordinate. Those mechanics are set down later, together with union.

## Bonds

The third variable is the medium. A bond is two beings tied together to a third thing. It is not the two tied to each other. They are joined because both are tied to the same thing.

So a bond is independent of both the axis of distance and the axis of ties. There is contemplation with a bond and without one, union with a bond and without one. A bond holds even between strangers. To be tied to an idea is, followed to the end, to be tied to the people who speak that idea together. So people of one idea are tied to one medium without knowing each other. Yet several lines hung on one post do not make those who hung them hold each other up. Support arises when each becomes the end of the other's line. A bond is not support but alignment. Tied to the same thing, they face the same way.

## The Pull and the Line

A being has an empty place, the hollow (空洞). The hollow pulls what can fill it. Call that pull hunger. The hollow is filled either by the touch of what fills it or by being tied with a line. When the other is a being and the filling comes by touch, that touch is union. A line sometimes holds a union in place, and sometimes fills the hollow without union.

There are three forms. Union with a line is filled by touch and bound. When the two come apart, it tightens. Union without a line is filled by touch and not bound. When the two come apart, it does not tighten. A line without union is bound without touching. Being tied to an idea or to the dead belongs here. That line too grew from the pull of the hollow. Only it is the hollow filled not with a being but with a line.

A line that binds beings in union is the pull turned into binding. The hollow is already filled by the interlocking, yet the two are bound so that the union lasts apart from the pull. If a union without a line lasts, it is not because of a line. The two lie against each other by the pull, or for some other reason. Union with a line and union without one part when things shake. One tightens and one is pulled. What tightens when shaken is a line, and the pull is something else. From outside one sees that the union lasts through the shaking; whether a line or the pull is keeping it is felt from inside.

The mechanics of one-sided subordination come from here. There is a union in which only one side is filled. The hollow of one being is filled by the other, while the hollow of the other is not filled by the first. The hunger of the first pulls the other, and the hunger of the second does not. The pull between the two runs one way. If a line is fastened to keep that union, it hangs only on the side that is filled. This is what it means that subordination is not a matter of high and low but direction.

The pull has an opposite direction as well. Not one's own hollow pulling what would fill it, but being pulled toward another's hollow. Wanting to fill. That direction is not set down here; only its place is kept.

## Union

While interlocked, two beings withstand more, by as much as each other's weight. And the place where they interlock changes. The strength of holding and the pain of interlocking come from the same place. Two tied to each other share the vibration. Two interlocked do not share it. Being in the same place, they shake together. Pushed over, they fall together.

The feeling of having become one lasts while the two are interlocked. It feels short because beings change. For two beings to keep interlocking while each changes, the two changes have to match: in a direction that keeps the fit, and at the same speed. That is hard, and the harder it is, the shorter it feels. When it goes, the two changes have fallen out of step, apart from the strength of the union or of the pull. Vibration breaks a union not by wearing down weight but by putting shapes out of step.

Oneness is a mode and the feeling of oneness is a feeling. What there is between beings with selves is the latter. For two to become one is for their motion to become one. However deeply they interlock, they are two as long as a moment remains when pushing this one does not bring that one along. Two that fall together when pushed are still two, and after the fall they lie apart. Between beings with selves that moment does not go away. So the union of beings with selves is always a union of two, and the feeling of having become one is a feeling.

Friction arises as interlocked frames fall out of step. The shapes of beings differ from one another and keep changing. A union without friction is rare. Friction is not the failure of union but the evidence of it.

What a union leaves behind divides in two. The impact is irreversible and the shape is reversible. A place made concave by interlocking can later be filled by something else. But the event of that place having been made is not erased. When a union comes undone, the place that interlocked keeps the shape of the other. The distance changes and the altered shape remains.

As said above, a union without friction is rare. Yet it can be possible in a union where one side has stopped. There are two ways of stopping: cutting the other to fit one's own shape, and cutting oneself to fit the other's. Call the first possession and the second dodder. Dodder is the name of a plant that sinks its suckers into a neighboring plant and lives without leaves or roots of its own. The side that was cut no longer collides, its shape having changed, and the side that did the cutting fits exactly, its shape being as it was.

There is a drive in which both sides try to make the two into one. When it is a union between people, it is also called mad love (狂戀). It aims at a one that cannot be. Becoming one is motion becoming one, and since that cannot happen between beings with selves, the drive, when it is strong, cuts at least one of the two. Mad love is not the limit of union but its destruction. It is trying to blend past the distance at which two can lie against each other. That name is given from outside. Inside a person it is the most intense love. Because becoming one is impossible, it is taken for a miracle and becomes the stuff of literature.

## Friction

Between beings that change there are three kinds of mechanics: tightening, friction, and going back and forth. Tightening arises in the line when things shake. Friction arises where there is touch when things fall out of step. Going back and forth is the passage between contemplation and union.

A line tightens and a union rubs. Far apart it is tension (張力); in contact it is friction. A difference in distance translates into a difference in mechanics. When a hollow that a line was fastened to fills up, the line rubs the flesh. Friction grows as the two draw near and is greatest in union. That the bumps and dents interlock means the touching surface is that much wider. Some picture union as the end of friction: once close, the colliding will be over. But union is the peak of friction. Colliding is the evidence of having come close.

Friction is a function of how much the shapes collide and how hard they are pressed. How far out of step the two sets of bumps and dents interlock, and how hard they are pushed together. Even where they interlock well, friction is large if they are pressed hard; even where they interlock badly, it is small if they touch lightly.

What friction leaves sometimes keeps a union. Two shapes worn by friction come to fit each other. Deeply interlocked bumps and dents do not come loose even with no force pulling them together. This is how an old union lasts with neither pull nor line. The shapes have fitted into each other and do not come out. Two shapes deeply interlocked are each other's mold. A being joined that way may be unable to stand once the other breaks, or may stand only once the other breaks, or may come away and stand before the breaking. When one side breaks, the one left either cannot stand because the place cut to the other's shape is now empty, or stands in its own shape for the first time because the place pressed down by the other is released. Which it will be is not known before the breaking. And in the latter two cases the standing is not immediate. A shape that was pressed down takes time to return.

## Contemplation

Contemplation is not altering. Being worn by friction is not alteration. Wear is both sides wearing down, so it is not one side correcting the other; alteration is one side cutting the other, so it has a direction.

When two beings at a distance draw near, they often contemplate each other's movement. How the other moves, where it is concave and where convex. Watching so, they come closer little by little, and the distance shrinks as each receives where the other juts out. There are also those who join without contemplation, like a disaster. The pull is not contemplation. A hollow needs no eye to pull. So contemplation is not a condition of union; union after contemplation and union without it both exist.

Nor does passing through contemplation make a union smoother. Friction is set by how much the two shapes collide where they interlock, and contemplation cannot change a shape. What contemplation gave shows not in oneness but in friction. An eye that reads friction. The eye that has seen the other's shape and movement lets one recall and read the whole of the other, which cannot be seen at the moment of friction, even while one is too close to see.

Contemplation as a mode and contemplation as an eye are different. Contemplation as a mode, seeing shape and movement whole, comes about where there is distance. Contemplation as an eye is the knowledge that the other has a far side one does not see, and the habit of reading friction not as the collision of a moment but as a trajectory. With what was seen before interlocking and with that eye, one reads the friction of the interlocked place.

The chance for contemplation lies inside the going back and forth of union. Union is a state, so with distance it can form and come apart, back and forth. Two beings see the whole of each other not at the moment they lie against each other but when there is distance. The phase of being apart is the place of contemplation. A relation with no phase of being apart is a relation without contemplation: two so close that there is no moment when the other is over there. The chance for contemplation comes when there is distance between beings. Going back and forth gives each the chance to see the other from an angle not seen before.

The contemplative eye also tells one friction from another. It tells whether this friction is coexistence or cutting. Whether the two are wearing down so as to stay two at close range, or one is correcting the other into its own shape. When friction is large, that eye also tells whether the shapes are out of step or the pressing is hard. Shapes do not change, and pressing comes from the pull and from lines. Knowing which it is, is the work of the eye.

The far side is not seen. Contemplating does not let one see a side that is not seen. But when the other moves, there are moments when the far side turns around and becomes a side that is seen, and seeing that moment belongs to the contemplative eye. Both the knowledge that there is a far side and the seeing of the moment it turns into view come from contemplation.

## Nearness

The nearer two are, the larger even a small bump touches. From far away only the large bumps show; up close even the grain touches. Whether there is touch is set by distance, and how much is set by the size of the bump together with distance. Contact is at once a wound and warmth. A bump on a being that would not have touched from far away touches large once that being is near. The next move differs by whether one puts it down to the size of the bump or to the distance, and by whether one looks at the wound or at the warmth.
