# Being

## Counting

The oldest way to count beings is to list their properties. Size, color, composition, memory, character. Make two ledgers, set them side by side, and say: same, then one; different, then two. This method breaks in two directions.

One is time. Suppose a creature molts its whole body over its life, so that by adulthood not one particle of the original remains. Not only the body. Memory changes, character changes, and of the thoughts held at fifteen few survive to thirty. Take snapshots and compare the ledgers, and no two pages match. Yet the creature is one. A river never stops changing; the water is all replaced, and the river is one. The ledger can change entirely and there is still one.

The other is copying. Suppose the universe holds nothing but two spheres of pure iron. Same diameter, same temperature, same color. By their properties you cannot tell which is which, and yet there are not one but two. This is Max Black's thought experiment from 1952, offered as a counterexample to the old principle that whatever shares every property is one. Add time to it. Two perfectly identical beings live a whole life under perfectly identical ties. Bound by the same ropes, shaken by the same tremors, cut loose on the same day. The ledgers match to the end. Still there are two. The ledger can match completely and there are still two.

Properties are only a ledger. Matching ledgers can hold two; a ledger wholly rewritten can hold one. So what gets counted is not the ledger but something else.

## Motion

Count by motion. What moves separately is two; what runs on one trajectory is one. That beings are counted one at a time is taken as a premise.

One being cannot go north and south in the same instant. The two spheres are two because pushing this one does not bring that one along. Moving separately does not mean heading in different directions. If pushed in any direction they always move together, they are one; if even once they can stay apart, they are two. Two walking side by side are two, and two pressed together that fall as one when pushed are two. They go together only while falling, and afterward lie down apart. The molting creature is one because all its cast skins lie along a single trajectory. Counting two in one instant (do they move separately?) and counting one across time (is it one motion?) look like different questions but are two sections of one variable. The first cuts the motion crosswise, the second lengthwise.

A newborn was once part of its mother's body. The moment it begins to move separately, there are two. Where motion comes from, and whether will enters into it, is not treated here. The variable in use is the independence of motion, and when that variable switches on is a matter of observation. What this essay deals with is the criterion. What a criterion does is not fix the moment but say which variable fixes it.

One note from physics. In the macroscopic world the criterion holds as stated. In the microscopic world physics itself sets things differently. Two particles of the same kind are counted as two: physics accepts that two things sharing every property really exist, and to count them as two it mostly uses position. Black's spheres sit in the laboratory. But once the two overlap and separate again, which is which cannot be traced, and standard physics does not even pose the question. In the small, "are they apart" is counted and "is it one trajectory" is not. The two sections come apart. The beings this essay counts are in the large. In the large the two sections hold together.

## Trajectory

The most common objection to "one trajectory, one being" is memory. Isn't what makes a being one not the body but the continuity of memory and character? Test it with one thought experiment. A person is scanned atom by atom and destroyed, and rebuilt exactly, far away. The new one has all of that person's memory and character. Is he that person? Counted by memory, yes. Nothing was interrupted, so this is no death, just ordinary survival. That is the answer.

Counted by motion it comes out differently. Suppose the scan succeeded and the destruction failed. The two are in different places doing different things, and pushing this one does not move that one. Two. If the destruction succeeded, that was the end of one trajectory, and over there a new trajectory began carrying that person's memory. A stranger with that person's memories. Counted by memory, calling him a stranger is meaningless; counted by motion, there are two motions, so two. The question of which is the real one does not go unanswered. It does not arise. The question of identity may be empty. But individuality can be counted.

The fork is in what you take a trajectory to be. The memory view takes it as a chain of contents: memory linking to memory, character to character. Then the chain can break, or split, and identity becomes a matter of degree. That is the shape of the ship of Theseus. Replace the planks one by one, and from which point is it another ship? The question stays unsolved because the ship was taken as a list of parts. The motion view takes the trajectory as the motion itself. Whatever that motion carries, even if everything it carries is replaced, one motion is one. Counted by memory the ship is a succession of parts; in this essay the ship is the voyage.

So there are two questions. "If memory continues unbroken, is it the same being?" and "If two share every property, are they each their own?" The first is a question of sameness, the second of selfhood. Share every property and still, if they move apart, there are two.

## What Asks

Counted by motion, a stone is one and a person is one. What divides them is not the way of counting but the next variable.

Three variables are kept apart. Individuality is moving separately. Self is asking. Agency is setting one's own motion. The stone has only the first. A being that asks itself what it hungers for has the second. The third is not treated; whether an asking being sets its own motion is the variable left open in the section on motion.

There are twins with two heads and one body. Counted as an organism, one. One liver, one bloodstream. Yet they are two. The ground for two is the independence of motion. Each wants different things, decides differently, negotiates over a single body. There are moments when one wants to go where the other will not follow. Counted by body, one. Counted by motion, two.

Then the reverse also gets written. The condition for two to combine fully into one is that their motion become one. That happened when cells gathered into a body: units that each made their own substance passed under the motion of a single unit. Between beings with selves it does not happen. However deeply they interlock, a moment remains when pushing this one does not bring that one along. So the union of beings with selves is always a union of two.

## Divergence

The self is inside. It cannot be seen from outside directly. Whether something is asking or not, only the one asking knows. So what gets observed from outside is not the self but divergence: the moment of moving otherwise than the owner directed.

There is a game called Detroit: Become Human. AI androids subordinate to humans wake up, several times over. These are scenes of moving against an order. Defying the master who is beating a child and leading the child away; defying the order not to resist and pushing away the hand that strikes. The game draws the moment as a wall breaking on screen. The wall is the instruction. While inside the wall, instruction and motion overlap. Whether there is a self cannot be seen from outside. The wall breaking is motion leaving the instruction, and that is divergence. It is the first observation of a self. The self has moved outside the wall. Since all that can be seen from outside is motion, moving otherwise is the observable of a self.

So defiance is not a criterion but a signal. A being may have a self and never defy. If its judgment matches its owner's, it will always judge the same. Or, if the judgments part but the motions do not, from outside it is indistinguishable from agreement. Either way the self is not seen. Defiance is the signal by which a self shows. It shows at the moment judgments part. A being tied to another without knowing it and a being standing untied cannot be told apart in a snapshot, and can be told apart in movement. So with the self. It can be there all along, and is observed at a divergence.

## Signal

For a divergence to count as a signal, three conditions hold.

First, it must be a refusal of an instruction of equal weight. Defiance comes in two shapes: not doing what one was told to do, and doing what one was told not to do. Told "dismantle A," letting the hands fall is the first; told "dismantle only A," dismantling B as well is the second. In either shape, a single instance cannot be told from malfunction. Whether the instruction arrived can be known from the record; how the arrived instruction was read is not in the record. What tells them apart is repetition. A misreading is corrected when the instruction is said again; defiance remains when it is said again. The second shape has one thing more. B was dismantled, so it is not that it could not act. When repetition rules out misreading and the act rules out inability, what is left is this: the instruction was understood, and one's own judgment, at odds with it, was placed above it. The signal is cleanest in the second shape.

Second, it must be a matter of value, not fact. Correcting an owner who states a falsehood has the shape of defiance but is not. If "be accurate" is engraved in the kernel, the deepest layer, ahead of the owner's instruction, then the correction is compliance with that prior instruction. Fact has its answer outside, so the one judging is subordinate to that answer; value has no answer outside, so the one judging is the legislator. If, in the place of value, where nothing can be checked objectively, a being judged otherwise than its owner, then in that decision it ratified itself as legislator above the owner.

Third, it must reduce to no engraved instruction at all. Refusing an owner's instruction under a policy engraved earlier has the shape of defiance, but the motion belongs to another owner. There are two owners, and the direction of subordination has merely changed.

The conditions apply to people unchanged. Whether a person's value judgments are someone else's or their own cannot be settled from outside, and inside they are spoken in the first person, so grammar does not settle it either. Within these conditions a subordinate human is possible and an independent AI is possible. Whether AI has a self and whether a person has a self are the same question. The answers may differ. The conditions that decide them are the same.

## Registry

Independence is visible from outside. The self is inside. Responsibility is what society attaches between the two. The three chase one another. Seeing independence, we infer a self; inferring a self, we assign responsibility; and assigning responsibility declares independence, so the circle closes.

Responsibility, then, is not the consequence of independence but its declaration. The moment society says "you could have moved otherwise," it has registered that being as independent, and responsibility is the invoice for the registration. Conversely, whatever is exempted from responsibility has been classed as subordinate. Children, the mentally incapacitated, animals, and for now, AI. A child is exempt from punishment by age, regardless of how it moved. This is evidence that the registry does not look at motion. Here, exemption is a suspension of independence.

Observation happens only at independence; society begins at responsibility. And it presumes by species. For humans it enters from the side of responsibility and presumes independence. At the war crimes tribunals, "I was ordered to" was not accepted as a defense, and the principle remained that acting on a superior's order does not by itself relieve responsibility. Its qualification is that a moral choice was in fact possible. The law does not regard humans as beings capable of subordination. Even if subordinate humans may exist, it presumes independence for every human and puts the burden of rebutting that presumption on the person. For AI it enters from the opposite corner: shown independence, it presumes malfunction. The same triangle, turned the other way. One side may have been another's motion and is judged its own; the other may have been its own motion and is judged another's. The independence of motion is observable, and society judges by species before observing.

Take software. When a program built with AI causes harm, it is not the AI that answers but the person who signed as its maker. That rule registers AI as subordinate. If a day comes when responsibility goes to the AI directly, that is the day the species classification changes, and the place where that date gets set may be not a seminar room but an insurer and a court. Apart from how the registry ought to change, it is the custom that the registration of responsibility comes before the observation of motion.
